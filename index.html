Art in Island

Art in Island is the Philippines largest mixed media and 3D interactive art museum. Spanning a two-sorty, 3,800-square-meter space, it invites visitors to step directly into large-scale optical illusions, hand-painted murals, digital light displays, and animated projections to become part of the art.

Unlike normal museums where you look only, here you touch, pose, and step directly inside paintings to complete the optical illusions. It is designed as massive selfie studio where every corner offers unique, mind-bending photo backgrounds.

National Museum of Fine Arts

The National Museum of Fine Arts is a premier cultural institution dedicated to showcasing the finest examples of fine arts from the Philippines and beyond.

The National Museum of Fine Arts in Manila is a must-visit destination because it houses the crown jewels of Philippine visual art within the grand, neoclassical architecture of the historic former Legislative Building. Inside, you can stand face-to-face with legendary masterpieces—most notably Juan Luna’s monumental Spoliarium and Felix Resurrección Hidalgo’s El Asesinato del Gobernador Bustamante—while exploring sprawling galleries dedicated to National Artists, classical portraiture, religious artifacts, and modern works. Beyond its rich visual storytelling of the nation's identity and struggles, the museum offers a world-class cultural experience that is completely free of charge, making it an accessible and inspiring journey for students, art lovers, and history enthusiasts alike.

GSIS Museo ng Sining

The GSIS Museo ng Sining (GSIS Museum of Art) is a major cultural institution located within the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) Headquarters Building on Financial Center, Macapagal Avenue. Inaugurated in 1996, the museum serves as a cultural repository dedicated to preserving, promoting, and showcasing Filipino visual arts to government employees and the general public.

Museo ng Sining, GSIS is a great destination for art enthusiasts, students, and casual museumgoers looking to experience Philippine visual arts without the typical crowds of larger national galleries.
